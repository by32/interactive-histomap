Who is who

    Colours distinguish formations, not documented uniforms. Each figure stands for 5 soldiers. Greek numbers follow Herodotus 7.202–203; the Persian host is drawn at 30,000 and its true size is disputed.

    About this reconstruction

    The terrain is a schematic model built from the ancient descriptions, not survey data. The coastline follows the geological reconstruction of Kraft, Rapp, Szemler, Tziavos & Kase (Journal of Field Archaeology 14, 1987), which places the 480 BC shore within tens of metres of the cliffs; today's plain is river silt. The today switch swaps in that plain with its coast 3–5 km further north, the Spercheios, the roads and the 1955 monument, and dashes the other era's shoreline across each view. The exact course of the Anopaea path is still debated. Narration follows Herodotus 7.175–239 with Diodorus 11.4–11 and Plutarch where noted.

    THE MATERIAL RECORD

    The art behind the reconstruction

    Objects made in the world of Thermopylae. These surviving works inform the equipment and visual language of the scene.

    Evidence and interpretation

    The Greek figures carry round shields, thrusting spears and Corinthian-type helmets. Persian figures wear sleeved tunics, trousers and soft caps, with wicker shields and bows, following Herodotus 7.61. Colours distinguish armies; they are not known uniforms. The Susa archers are palace imagery, and the vase is not a documented scene of Thermopylae.

    The mountain and ancient shore form a schematic reconstruction, not a surveyed landscape. The path’s exact course is debated. Three days are compressed into 3 minutes 12 seconds. Cuts skip elapsed time; combat movements, figure sizes and losses are illustrative, not measured casualty counts.

    The fighting follows Herodotus 7.210–212; the final advance, death of Leonidas and retreat follow 7.223–225. His description of the Thebans’ motives is his interpretation; their separation and surrender are recounted in 7.233. The relief atlas and the 3D view use the same sequence.
